Reasoning effort

What the effort bar changes

Official model guide ↗

Higher effort gives the selected model more room for planning, analysis, and checking. It often takes longer and may generate more output tokens, so a task may use more credits. The model's rate per token stays the same.

Light / LowQuick, well-scoped work
MediumBalanced depth and speed
High / Extra HighMore difficult multi-step work
MaxMore time for one hard task
UltraUses subagents for separable work; usage can rise

OpenAI suggests starting with Astra Light, Sol Medium, or Luna High. Effort levels do not map exactly between model generations, and there is no fixed cost or time multiplier for each step.

Worth knowing

Price versus plan usage

The rates above estimate credit use from local token counts. Your 5-hour and weekly plan meters also depend on model, context, reasoning, tools, caching, and other activity. A lower token rate does not guarantee that every prompt uses less of your plan.

GPT‑5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna remain available during the GPT‑6 rollout. OpenAI has not published a Terra retirement date. GPT‑5.5 retires from ChatGPT and Codex on October 14, 2026.

Interpretation and choice

What should you use?

Overall: GPT‑6 Sol had slightly more calls in the median turn, yet a lower median credit estimate. Its lower per-token rates mattered more in this mix of work.

Same-effort check: At Medium, the medians were 6.38 credits across 860 GPT‑5.6 Sol turns and 12.10 across 46 GPT‑6 Sol turns. But 83% of the GPT‑6 Medium turns came from one task, so this does not isolate a model effect.

Why some prompts burn credits: The 10+ call band dominates estimated credits for both models. The millions of cached tokens are repeated reads across calls—not one context window. GPT‑6 had a larger share of turns in that band, though its median credits within each band were lower in this uneven sample.

Practical choice: GPT‑6 Sol is not automatically more expensive per turn, nor is its lower rate a guarantee. For bounded work, specify files, desired output, and when to report before expanding. For open-ended work, extra iterations may be worthwhile; monitor model calls and cached input in Task / Session. This sample cannot establish which model is intrinsically faster or better quality.
