Sol vs Terra: what a whole turn used

The rate table above prices tokens. This informal sample shows what happened across complete turns, including repeated model calls and tool use.

What happened: Sol made more model and tool calls before reporting back. Its typical turn took longer and used more estimated credits, despite its lower output-token rate.

Why: More iterations can bring earlier context and tool results into later model calls. Long-running commands can also extend elapsed time without measuring model speed.

The choice: Extra investigation may be worth the time and usage on open-ended work. For bounded work, a shorter, more predictable turn may suit you better.

Sample: 12 completed Terra turns immediately before the switch in each task, then all 41 completed Sol turns after it. What the effort bar changes

Official model guide ↗

Higher effort gives the selected model more room for planning, analysis, and checking. It often takes longer and may generate more output tokens, so a task may use more credits. The model's rate per token stays the same.

Light / LowQuick, well-scoped work
MediumBalanced depth and speed
High / Extra HighMore difficult multi-step work
MaxMore time for one hard task
UltraUses subagents for separable work; usage can rise

OpenAI suggests starting with Astra Light, Sol Medium, or Luna High. Effort levels do not map exactly between model generations, and there is no fixed cost or time multiplier for each step.

Worth knowing

Price versus plan usage

The rates above estimate credit use from local token counts. Your 5-hour and weekly plan meters also depend on model, context, reasoning, tools, caching, and other activity. A lower token rate does not guarantee that every prompt uses less of your plan.

GPT‑5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna remain available during the GPT‑6 rollout. OpenAI has not published a Terra retirement date. GPT‑5.5 retires from ChatGPT and Codex on October 14, 2026.
